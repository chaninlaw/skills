# Thailand specifics

Apply when `seller.md` jurisdiction is Thailand. These are drafting pointers to verify, not legal advice; check current rates and rules before relying on them.

- **Contract type**: custom software work is generally a hire-of-work contract (สัญญาจ้างทำของ) under the Civil and Commercial Code; MA is often a service agreement alongside it.
- **Governing law and disputes**: Thai law; Thai courts, or arbitration (e.g. the Thai Arbitration Institute or THAC) if both sides want it.
- **IP**: copyright under the Copyright Act B.E. 2537. In commissioned work, who owns copyright depends on the contract terms, so state the assignment and the vendor's retained components in writing.
- **Personal data**: PDPA B.E. 2562. When the system processes the client's customers' data, the client is usually the controller and the vendor the processor; add processing terms (purpose, security, sub-processors, breach notice, deletion at end).
- **Electronic signatures**: generally valid under the Electronic Transactions Act B.E. 2544; agree the signing method in the contract.
- **Tax**:
  - VAT 7% applies only if the seller is VAT-registered.
  - Juristic-person clients usually withhold 3% on service fees paid to Thai residents and issue a withholding certificate (หนังสือรับรองการหักภาษี ณ ที่จ่าย). Show it on the quotation and invoice.
  - The rate depends on whether the payee is an individual or a company, so check it.
- **Stamp duty**: hire-of-work contracts may attract stamp duty (อากรแสตมป์). Agree who pays it.
- **Late-payment interest**: follow the Civil and Commercial Code default-interest rules, or state a contractual rate within legal limits.
- **Language**: if both Thai and English versions exist, state which one prevails.
